# Module 1: How AI Works – Transcript

[SLIDE 1: Title – HOOK]

**HOOK**

The AI tool you use for your studies or your work has never once checked whether anything it tells you is true. And yet it sounds more confident than most people you know.

[SLIDE 2: The puzzle – INTRIGUE]

**INTRIGUE**

How can that be? There is one simple idea that explains it, and once you have it, you will read every AI answer differently. You do not need any technical background. If you can use the predictive text on your phone, you already know most of what you need. Let me show you.

[SLIDE 3: It predicts the next word – DELIVERY]

**DELIVERY**

Type "See you" into your phone and it might offer "later" or "soon". It has not understood your plans. It has simply noticed which words tend to follow which.

Tools such as ChatGPT, Copilot and Claude do the same kind of thing, on a vastly greater scale. Given the words so far, the tool works out which word, or fragment of a word, is most likely to come next. It adds that word, then repeats the calculation for the one after. And again. And again. An entire essay, poem or piece of computer code is built one small step at a time.

[SLIDE 4: Trained on patterns, not on the world – DELIVERY]

So how does it know what is likely? It has been trained on an enormous collection of text: books, articles, websites, discussion forums and more. During training, the system is shown passages with words hidden and asked to guess them. When it guesses wrongly, its internal settings are nudged very slightly. Repeat that billions of times, and the system becomes extraordinarily good at reproducing the patterns of human writing.

Imagine someone who has read an immense library, but who was never allowed to visit the places described, meet the people, or check a single fact. They would know a great deal about how people write about the world, and much less about the world itself. That is a helpful picture of what these tools are.

[SLIDE 5: Fluent is not the same as true – DELIVERY]

Here is the key point. The tool is aiming for text that is plausible, not text that is true.

Most of the time the two overlap, because most of what people write is broadly accurate. But where the training material is thin, mixed or contradictory, the tool still produces something that sounds right. It has no inner alarm that says, "I am not sure about this." So it can invent a reference, a quotation or a statistic, and deliver it with exactly the same calm confidence as a basic fact.

You will often hear this called a "hallucination", which makes it sound like a rare glitch. It is better to think of it as the normal process working as designed: a convincing sentence that happens not to be correct.

[SLIDE 6: What this means for you – DELIVERY]

So what does this mean for you? Three things.

First, treat fluency as a style, not as evidence. A polished paragraph tells you the tool is skilled with language. It tells you nothing about whether the content is accurate.

Second, the way you ask shapes what you get. The tool follows the pattern your words set up, so a loaded question or a hidden assumption will steer the answer. Ask about "the myth of" something and you will get a very different response from asking about "the cause of" it.

Third, you remain the person who knows, or who finds out. The tool is not weighing whether it is right, so someone has to, and that someone is you.

**EXIT**
