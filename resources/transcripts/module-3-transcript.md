# Module 3: Characteristics of AI Outputs – Transcript

[SLIDE 1: Title – HOOK]

**HOOK**

You can often feel when a piece of writing was produced by AI. But can you say why? And would you stake a student's reputation on it?

[SLIDE 2: The puzzle – INTRIGUE]

**INTRIGUE**

Here is the surprising part. The features that make AI text feel odd are not the ones that should worry you most. The real dangers are quieter, and they are hiding in text that reads beautifully. In the next few minutes, I will give you four things to look for, and a simple audit you can apply to any piece of AI output, including your own drafts.

[SLIDE 3: Confident and wrong – DELIVERY]

**DELIVERY**

The first characteristic is fluent inaccuracy. AI-generated text is almost always smoothly written, well punctuated and confident in tone. That polish is exactly what makes errors hard to spot. A wrong date, an invented quotation or a statistic with no real source will sit in the middle of a perfectly sensible paragraph, looking no different from the correct material around it.

So do not let the quality of the writing lower your guard. Ask of any specific claim: where does this come from, and could I find it?

[SLIDE 4: The missing trail – DELIVERY]

The second characteristic is a missing trail. In scholarly writing, a claim usually points somewhere: to a study, a dataset, a case, a named author. AI text often makes claims that float free. You will see phrases such as "research shows" or "experts agree" with no indication of which research or which experts. Treat each of these as a question mark. Either track down the real evidence, or leave the claim out.

[SLIDE 5: The comfortable middle – DELIVERY]

The third characteristic is sameness. Because the tool favours the most likely phrasing, its writing tends toward the average: stock openings that paint a broad picture, balanced sentences, lists of three, and cautious conclusions saying a "balanced approach" is needed. Real academic work often takes a position or makes a surprising connection. If a passage could appear in any essay on any topic, it probably tells you very little.

A caution is needed. Spotting a few of these habits does not prove that a person used AI, as many careful human writers produce similar prose. Style alone is poor evidence, so use these features to read critically, never to accuse.

[SLIDE 6: Whose view of the world? – DELIVERY]

The fourth characteristic is bias. The tool learned from text that is not a fair sample of humanity. A great deal of it is in English and reflects wealthier, Western and online perspectives. As a result, the tool may treat some viewpoints as the default, underrepresent voices from other regions or languages, or repeat stereotypes it absorbed. Bias often sits in what is left out: whose examples are used, and whose work is never mentioned. "Who is missing from this answer?" is a powerful check.

[SLIDE 7: A simple audit – DELIVERY]

Putting these together, here is a four-question audit. What exactly is being claimed? What is the evidence for each claim? What is the tone doing, and where is it vague or padded? And whose perspective is shown, and whose is missing? Then verify the claims that matter against reliable sources before you rely on them.
